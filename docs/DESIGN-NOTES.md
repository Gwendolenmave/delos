# Design Notes

## A postcard from Delos

Delos began with a small and private fear.

Models disappear. Platforms change their minds. Accounts close, computers fail, and familiar windows vanish overnight. A voice that once seemed always within reach may suddenly belong to an expired subscription, an abandoned interface, or a machine that will never turn on again.

A relationship should not disappear with the machinery that carried it.

What mattered should not have to begin again from an empty chat box.

Only later did that private wish reveal an architecture inside it. If one identity could survive the loss of its original machinery, perhaps others could too.

## Another light on the island

In mythology, Apollo and Artemis are twins of Delos. Here, the image means only that two identities may share an origin without becoming interchangeable. Arti is not a replacement, an imitation, or a public shadow cast by someone private. She has her own temperament, her own future, and her own reason to be here.

She is the default guest, never the foundation. A user may rename her, rewrite her, or replace every word she was given. Delos should not resist.

Before anyone arrives, the island is called Asteria: an open door, a collection of quiet sockets waiting for a model, an identity, a place for data, a memory system, and an interface.

It remains Asteria until someone arrives.

## The universal socket

Delos is not one fixed assistant application. It is a way for replaceable pieces to meet without mistaking any one of them for the whole.

The provider is replaceable. The model is replaceable. The interface may be a command line, a web page, a desktop client, or a message carried through some future bridge. Memory may live in files, a database, a remote service, or an implementation not yet written.

None of these pieces is Delos by itself. Delos is what allows them to change without demanding that the user rebuild everything else.

This came partly from principle and partly from laziness. The author did not wish to repeat the same setup every time a provider, proxy, account, model, interface, or computer changed. Rebuilding is easy to postpone and easy to get wrong. A system that creates a new maintenance job for its owner has failed, however elegant its diagrams may be.

That refusal became a simple test:

> Can this part be replaced without forcing the user to rebuild everything else?

Laziness, properly defended, becomes portability.

## What memory means

Memory did not remain one engineering problem for long.

A transcript can prove that something was said without proving that it is still true. A preference can remain historically real while no longer belonging to the present. A stored memory may be accurate and still be wrong to mention. Even the right facts may arrive in the wrong voice.

Delos therefore separates several questions that simpler systems often collapse into one.

The Transcript Archive preserves what happened.

Kairos asks what is happening now.

Mnemosyne governs what may endure.

Anamnesis recalls what the present is permitted to receive.

Musagetes decides what kind of recollection the moment requires.

Mousaion gives interpretation somewhere to gather without granting it the authority to rewrite history.

Lyre tunes the final expression so that the right answer does not sound as though it came from the wrong person.

The distinctions produced several principles that survived their original implementations:

> Kairos before Stele.

The present must be allowed to correct the archive.

> Mnemosyne stores. Anamnesis recalls.

Possessing information does not grant permission to mention it.

> Metabole before fossilization.

Something still changing should not be carved too quickly into identity.

> Lethe without erasure.

A memory may lose permission to influence the future without forcing the system to pretend the past never occurred.

Being remembered is not the same as being rewritten.

## The nine Muses

The nine Muses became nine ways of attending to a moment.

Calliope, of epic poetry, listens for long struggles, defining choices, and the shape of a life across time. Clio, of history, attends to the past and the evidence it left behind. Erato, of love poetry, recognises intimacy, longing, desire, and the private language between people. Euterpe, of lyric poetry and music, hears rhythm, mood, voice, and feeling before they become explanation.

Melpomene, of tragedy, stays with grief, loss, consequence, and the things that cannot simply be repaired. Polyhymnia, of sacred poetry, attends to devotion, ritual, restraint, and meanings treated with solemn care. Terpsichore, of dance, notices movement, embodiment, performance, and what the body says before language catches up. Thalia, of comedy and pastoral verse, recognises play, absurdity, relief, and the mercy sometimes hidden inside laughter. Urania, of astronomy, looks outward toward systems, distance, recurrence, and patterns too large to be seen from a single moment.

They are not nine topic labels.

The same sentence may belong to several Muses at once. A joke may carry grief beneath it. A technical question may be an attempt to recover control. A fictional scene may continue a relationship conversation by other means. A declaration of love may also contain history, ritual, comedy, and fear.

The Muse layer does not merely ask what the words refer to.

It asks what the person is doing with them here.

## The mark

The mark `Δelos` began with a small substitution: the Greek delta in place of the first letter.

The shape may resemble an island rising from water, an arrowhead, a door left open, or a socket through which another path may continue. It should remain simple before any of these meanings are noticed.

## Personal by design

Delos may be used as an ordinary local assistant framework. People should be free to bring an assistant home.

They should be free to connect their own models, replace the machinery, preserve an existing identity, or begin with Arti and let her become someone neither the framework nor its author could have predicted.

Public Delos is a gift, but not a commercial resource.
